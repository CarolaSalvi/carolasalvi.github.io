01 / IDEA

INSIGHT · CREATIVITY · COGNITIVE FLEXIBILITY

Creative cognition requires more than generating something new. It requires the ability to reorganize knowledge, explore alternatives, recognize promising ideas, and revise existing representations. My research investigates the cognitive and neurobiological mechanisms that support these transitions and their consequences for problem solving, learning, memory, and belief updating.

02 / RESEARCH

From Aha! to
mechanism.

My research traces insight across levels of analysis—from the insight accuracy effect and oculometric signatures of sudden solutions to their neural substrates. Using eye tracking and pupillometry, neuroimaging, and non-invasive brain stimulation, this work has identified converging markers of insight and demonstrated the causal contribution of the right temporal cortex to successful restructuring.

CAROLA SALVI’S BRAIN
01

Aha!

How an initial representation is reorganized and a previously inaccessible solution suddenly becomes available. This work examines attentional disengagement, eye and pupil markers, neural connectivity, and the causal contribution of brain systems involved in insight.

02

Insight, Learning & Memory

Why Aha! moments feel compelling, when they signal accuracy, and how insight changes what is remembered. This work connects the phenomenology of discovery with learning, reward, and adaptive updating.

03

Creative Cognition

How insight interacts with divergent and convergent thinking, associative search, incubation, and the emergence of original ideas. Current work examines multiple routes to creative ideation, including discontinuous insight-like transitions.

04

Cognitive Flexibility & Belief Updating

How reasoning and problem-solving processes relate to misinformation, conspiracy beliefs, overconfidence, and polarization—and how flexibility in restructuring representations may support adaptive evaluation of information.

03 / ABOUT

Cognitive
neuroscientist.

My scientific career has developed across Italy and the United States, moving between experimental psychology and cognitive neuroscience. I worked across academic, medical, and interdisciplinary research environments, including rehabilitation institutes and psychiatric departments. Working with neurological populations (e.g., individuals with focal brain lesions and Parkinson’s disease) brought a clinical dimension to my research and complemented experimental approaches with lesion mapping and the study of neuropsychological function. This trajectory has shaped a research approach that integrates behavioral experimentation with clinical and neuroscientific methods to investigate cognition across multiple levels of analysis.
